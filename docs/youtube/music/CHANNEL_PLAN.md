## Content options

| Direction | Core videos | Supporting videos |
|---|---|---|
| Original releases | Finished songs, performances, official audio or visualizers | Song introductions and brief previews |
| Making music | Writing, recording, arranging, production decisions, listening comparisons | Session clips and project progress |
| Both | Alternate a finished track with a making-of story | Short excerpts from the same project |

For a combined channel, one song can produce a small connected set: the finished track, its making-of episode, and a short excerpt. This is an efficient production option, not a claim that an unreleased song or footage is already available.

## Proposed playlists to select after the direction is confirmed

Keep artist names and track titles consistent. Decide artist versus label branding before drafting the final channel description or renaming anything. Do not invent genres, collaborators, release history, or audience claims.

## First production cycle

1. Identify the channel and choose its emphasis.
2. Select one existing owned track or a new song the owner wants to develop; record its actual stage.
3. Inventory the source session, usable audio, artwork, and footage. The owner can choose the files; none have been located in this planning task.
4. Choose one concrete outcome: publish-ready track video, making-of episode, or both.
5. Prepare the edit, title, description, credits, and thumbnail from the actual material.
6. Listen to the rendered output and verify the video/audio pairing, beginning/end, and intended export settings.
7. Choose a release date when the assets are ready. No automated schedule is configured.

If there is no finished song yet, begin with a studio introduction or the first real writing session instead of promising a release date.

## Candidate episode prompts

- “From a Simple Idea to a Full Track” — follow a real song from its first motif to the current arrangement.
- “Why I Changed This Part of the Song” — play the two versions and explain the musical choice.
- “Inside the Session: [Actual Track Title]” — show selected tracks and explain their roles.
- “Fixing [Actual Audio Problem]” — demonstrate a real issue, the change, and a listening comparison.
- “[Artist] — [Track Title]” — finished track with an agreed visual treatment.
- “Trying Our Own Tools in a Music Session” — show an actual working tool and what it contributes musically.

These are prompts, not finished scripts; titles and claims should follow the selected music.
